## Why an earlier version of this file said the opposite

This is recorded rather than deleted, because the question will be asked again.

The style was first built against the definition shipped by [DiceBear](https://www.dicebear.com/), whose
`meta.license` for avataaars reads **"Free for personal and commercial use"** with a link to
avataaars.com. That wording grants *use* and is silent on derivative works, so the style was held back as
a design study on the reasonable view that no redistribution right had been granted in writing.

That was wrong, and specifically it was **incomplete rather than misread**. The loose wording describes
the *Sketch source file* offered for download on avataaars.com. The same artwork was separately released
under MIT in `fangpenlin/avataaars`, and nothing on avataaars.com points at it. Finding the MIT grant
resolved the question; no permission was sought or needed.

## Provenance of the geometry, precisely

Two independent MIT grants cover what is in this repository, and it is worth being exact about which
one covers what, because the path data here did not come from `fangpenlin/avataaars` directly.

| Layer | What it is | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| Original artwork | Pablo Stanley's avataaars | MIT via `fangpenlin/avataaars` — Pablo Stanley named as copyright holder |
| Intermediate | DiceBear's remix, `@dicebear/styles` 10.2.0, from which the path data was taken | MIT, Copyright (c) 2026 Florian Körner |
| This style | `recipes/lexavataaars/` — DiceBear's shapes, filtered and extended | MIT (this repository) |

**Every piece used here exists in the MIT repository.** Verified file by file against
`fangpenlin/avataaars@93aa902`:

- All 20 kept hairstyles — `LongHairBigHair`, `LongHairBob`, `LongHairBun`, `LongHairCurly`,
  `LongHairCurvy`, `LongHairDreads`, `ShortHairDreads01`, `LongHairFro`, `LongHairNotTooLong`,
  `LongHairMiaWallace`, `ShortHairShortCurly`, `ShortHairShortFlat`, `ShortHairShortRound`,
  `ShortHairShortWaved`, `ShortHairSides`, `LongHairStraight`, `LongHairStraight2`,
  `LongHairStraightStrand`, `ShortHairTheCaesar`, `ShortHairTheCaesarSidePart`
- `clothes/BlazerShirt` — the garment every piece of tailoring here is built on
- The head, neck, nose, eyes, brow, mouth, spectacles and facial hair pieces retained

**One honest qualification.** The `d` strings in `source.g.ts` are DiceBear's optimised rendering of
those shapes, not byte copies of the TSX in `fangpenlin/avataaars`. DiceBear re-expressed the artwork on
a different canvas and flattened its masks. So the *shapes* are the MIT artwork; the *specific path data*
arrives via DiceBear's own MIT-licensed package. Both grants permit what this repository does, and the
chain does not depend on either one alone.

## Obligation that travels with the code

MIT requires the copyright notice and permission text to accompany the software. Three notices are
therefore required downstream and all three are reproduced in `NOTICE` at the repository root:

1. Copyright (c) 2017 Pablo Stanley, Fang-Pen Lin — the artwork
2. Copyright (c) 2026 Florian Körner — DiceBear, the path data as expressed here
3. This repository's own notice

Do not remove `NOTICE` from a distribution, and do not drop the upstream lines from it.

## What is borrowed and what is drawn

- `source.g.ts` — **borrowed**, generated by `extract.ts`. Every variant in it is byte-identical to
  DiceBear's, verified by comparing normalised element trees; 36 variants plus the head and neck.
- `hand.ts` — **drawn here**, though derived from the above: the garments are built on upstream's own
  shoulder arcs and neckline, and the added mouths, brows and eye are re-proportioned or repositioned
  upstream geometry. 15 variants.
- Palettes in the recipe are this project's, except `skin`, which is upstream's list minus two values.
